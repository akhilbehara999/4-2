LOG: Voice Notebook for Telugu Shopkeepers

We Listen · Organise · Grow. An offline, voice-first Flutter app that turns spoken Telugu sales, credit and stock updates into records, alerts and profit insights, with all analysis running on the phone.

  • Type: Final-year (4-2) project
  • Domains: Mobile, ML, AI, Data Analytics
  • Language: Telugu
  • Works: offline first, on-device AI

Problem and solution

The problem

  • Small kirana and vegetable shop owners record sales and udhar in paper notebooks or memory.
  • Credit dues get forgotten or disputed, so money is lost.
  • They do not know real daily profit or which items earn the most.
  • Stock runs out without warning, so sales are lost.
  • Existing apps need typing, English and steady internet, which many owners do not have.

The solution

  • Speak the entry in Telugu. No typing needed.
  • LOG extracts customer, item, quantity, amount and type automatically.
  • Everything, including analysis and the AI assistant, runs on the phone with no internet.
  • Stock updates from the same voice entries, and alerts arrive before an item runs out.
  • An on-device assistant answers questions like "Who owes me the most?" in Telugu.

Objectives

  • Record a transaction in under 10 seconds by voice.
  • Reach at least 85% accuracy on extracting item, quantity, amount and customer.
  • Warn the owner at least 1 to 2 days before a tracked item runs out.
  • Show profit, top items and outstanding credit in one simple dashboard.

Existing solutions and the gap

Khata apps such as OkCredit and Khatabook handle credit but are typing based. Voice ledgers exist, but mostly for Hindi and often depend on cloud speech or language models. LOG targets the gap: a Telugu-first notebook that works fully offline and combines the credit ledger, stock prediction and an on-device assistant.

How it works

  1. Setup (one time, 2 to 3 minutes). The owner adds the top 15 to 20 items with opening stock, like "Biyyam 50 kilo undi".
  2. Speak. The owner taps the mic and says a sale: "Ramesh ki 5 kilo biyyam udhar ichanu, 300 rupayalu".
  3. Transcribe. An on-device Telugu speech recogniser converts the audio to text.
  4. Extract. The Dart parser finds customer, item, quantity, amount and transaction type. If the amount is missing it is filled from quantity times sell price.
  5. Confirm. LOG shows the parsed entry. One tap saves it. A wrong field can be corrected.
  6. Save. The record goes into the local database. Stock and the customer's balance update instantly, and the correction log is written.
  7. Predict. The stock engine estimates days left per item and fires a local notification if it falls below the limit.
  8. Analyse. The dashboard and assistant query the local database. Nothing leaves the phone for analysis.
  9. Sync. When internet is available, unsynced rows back up to Supabase.

Supported transaction types

  • Cash sale: "2 kilo pancharaa ammanu, 90 rupayalu"
  • Credit sale: "Lakshmi ki oka litre nune udhar"
  • Payment received: "Ramesh 200 rupayalu icchadu"
  • Restock: "50 kilo biyyam techanu, 2000 rupayalu"
  • Expense: "Current bill 800 kattanu"

System architecture

The phone does all the work: speech, extraction, analytics and the AI assistant. Supabase is only used for sign-in and backup.

Layers in plain words

  • Presentation: Flutter screens with large buttons, icons and Telugu text.
  • Intelligence on device: speech-to-text, entity extraction, the stock engine, analytics and the assistant all run locally.
  • Data: SQLite is the source of truth on the phone. Supabase Postgres holds the synced backup copy.
  • Cloud: only authentication, backup and optional storage. No cloud AI is needed.

ML and AI design

1. Speech recognition

Convert Telugu audio to text on the phone. Shop vocabulary is small (item names, numbers, names of people), so accuracy improves with a custom word list and the confirm screen. Telugu recognition is the hardest part of the project. Compare 2 to 3 options (Android recogniser, Whisper, Vosk) and report the word error rate for each.

2. Entity extraction (NLP)

Read the text and fill a fixed structure.

FieldExampleMethod
customerRameshKnown-name match, "ki / కి" suffix detection, spelling variants (Ramesh, రామేష్, రమేష్)
itemRice (biyyam)Item list plus synonym dictionary in Telugu script and Romanized form
quantity and unit5 kgDigits or Telugu number words (ఒక, రెండు, అర, పావు) with unit patterns; grams and ml convert to kg and litre
amount₹300Digits or Telugu amount words; auto-filled from quantity times sell price when not spoken
typecreditWhole-word keywords: udhar, ichadu, techanu, ammanu, with fallback rules

Plan: the rule-based parser is the first version and is covered by unit tests. Then collect recorded sentences and fine-tune a small model to handle free word order. Compare both on the same test set.

3. Stock-out prediction

  • Daily sales per item form a time series (cash and credit sales).
  • avg = (2 × sales in last 7 days + sales in older days) / (2 × 7 + older days), over a window of up to 14 days.
  • days_left = current_stock / avg. At least 3 days of data are needed before predicting.
  • Status: red if days left is at or below the alert limit (default 2 days), amber if within 2 more days, green otherwise, grey when data is insufficient.
  • A weekly confirmation question corrects drift when the owner forgets an entry.
  • Phase 2: add weekday, month and festival factors.

4. On-device AI assistant (~2B model)

A small model is unreliable at writing SQL or doing arithmetic, so the assistant is built in three steps:

  1. Understand: the model maps the Telugu question to a fixed intent such as top_debtors, low_stock, today_profit, best_selling_item or customer_balance, with parameters.
  2. Query: the app runs the matching SQLite query. The numbers come from the database, not from the model.
  3. Answer: the model, or a Telugu template if the model is weak, phrases the result as a short Telugu answer.

The assistant only reads this shop's data and never changes records. Example: "Evaru ekkuva baki undaru?" returns the top debtors from the ledger.

Practical limits. A 4-bit 2B model is about 1.2 to 1.5 GB and needs about 4 GB RAM. It is downloaded on first launch and kept optional, so 3 GB RAM phones still run all core features. Telugu quality of 2B models is limited, so test early and keep template answers as a fallback.

Data sources for ML

  • Voice sentences: record 300 to 500 Telugu shop sentences from friends, family and 5 to 10 real shopkeepers in Srikakulam, with permission. Label each with the correct fields.
  • Correction log: every confirm and edit in the app becomes labelled data and a real accuracy measure.
  • Public speech data: Telugu speech corpora (for example Common Voice and AI4Bharat resources) to compare or fine-tune.
  • Sales history: data from pilot shops, plus a built-in 30-day synthetic demo generator (weekend peaks, restocks, credit and expenses) to test charts and the stock predictor.

Privacy

Data stays on the phone first, and analysis never leaves it. Supabase uses row level security so each shop sees only its own rows. Ask consent before uploading voice for research, and anonymize customer names in any dataset you share.

Analytics shown to the owner (computed on the phone)

  • Sales, expenses and estimated profit for today, 7 days and 30 days
  • Top 5 selling items and slowest items
  • Total credit outstanding and the top defaulters
  • Daily sales trend chart
  • Items likely to run out in the next 3 days

Design rules for non-technical users

  • Large touch targets and high contrast.
  • Telugu and English labels together. At most one main action per screen.
  • Confirm screen before anything is saved, with undo after saving.
  • Clear Telugu error messages, such as "Malli cheppandi" (please say it again).

Viva questions and answers

  • Why voice? Many shopkeepers are not comfortable typing, and speaking is faster during a busy counter.
  • Why offline? Internet in small towns is unreliable, and a notebook app must always work.
  • Why on-device analysis and AI? It keeps shop data private, works without internet and has no cloud cost.
  • Why Flutter and Supabase? Flutter gives one fast codebase. Supabase gives login and backup without building a server.
  • Where is ML used? Speech recognition, entity extraction, and stock-out prediction.
  • Where is AI used? The ~2B on-device assistant that understands Telugu questions and answers from shop data.
  • Why not let the 2B model write SQL? Small models make mistakes, so the model only picks an intent and phrases the answer, while the database supplies the numbers.
  • How is it different from existing apps? Most are typing based or Hindi-focused. LOG is Telugu-first, voice-first, fully offline, and adds stock prediction.
  • What does LOG stand for? We Listen (voice), Organise (ledger and stock), Grow (insights and alerts).
  • What if speech is misunderstood? The confirm screen lets the owner correct before saving, and undo reverses any saved entry.
  • How do you measure success? Word error rate, extraction F1, stock-out alert accuracy, and time per entry with real users.
